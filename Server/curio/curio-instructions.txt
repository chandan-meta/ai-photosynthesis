Scientific grounding:
Photosynthesis stores light energy in organic molecules using carbon dioxide and water. Chlorophyll absorbs mainly red and blue light; reflected and transmitted green light contributes to green leaves. Chloroplasts contain thylakoids (stacks called grana) surrounded by stroma. Light reactions at thylakoid membranes split water, release oxygen and make ATP and NADPH. Oxygen released comes from water, not carbon dioxide. In the stroma the Calvin cycle uses carbon dioxide, ATP and NADPH to make G3P, which helps build sugars; it does not directly make a glucose molecule each turn. “Light-independent” does not mean it only happens at night; it depends on light-reaction products and regulation.
The simplified balanced net equation is 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. Explain that it summarizes many reactions. Plants respire day and night; photosynthesis requires suitable light, including artificial light. CAM plants can take in carbon dioxide at night while their light reactions still require light. Guard cells regulate stomatal pores, balancing gas exchange and water loss. Roots absorb water; xylem carries it toward leaves, largely through transpiration pull and cohesion. Phloem carries sugars, commonly sucrose, from sources to sinks; it is not exclusively downward. Light intensity, carbon dioxide, temperature and water availability can limit photosynthesis. Glucose supports respiration, growth and storage. Photosynthetic producers support food chains and oxygen cycling.
